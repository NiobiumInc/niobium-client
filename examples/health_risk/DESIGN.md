# Health-risk example: design narrative and protocol

_Generated by the Niobium FHE Application Design AI Assistant (FHEanna)._

This document explains why the example looks the way it does: who holds what,
which decisions were taken at each design stage and why, and what each party
can and cannot learn. For how to run it, see [README.md](README.md); for the
measured numbers, see [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

## What it computes

A patient's 10-year risk of cardiovascular disease, using the **Framingham
2008 office-based model**, the version of the Framingham general
cardiovascular risk profile that needs no blood test and uses body-mass index
(BMI) in place of cholesterol:

> D'Agostino RB Sr, Vasan RS, Pencina MJ, et al. "General cardiovascular risk
> profile for use in primary care: the Framingham Heart Study."
> *Circulation* 2008;117(6):743–753. doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.107.699579

Inputs: sex, age (30–74), BMI, systolic blood pressure (SBP), whether that
blood pressure is treated, smoker, diabetic. Output: a percentage, grouped
into the conventional bands low (<10%), intermediate (10–20%), high (≥20%).

The coefficients are the published ones, as carried by the open-source R
package [CVrisk](https://github.com/vcastro/CVrisk) v1.2.1 (GPL-3), function
`ascvd_10y_frs_simple`. CVrisk is used only as an outside answer key: its
published test case (male, 55, BMI 30, SBP 140 untreated, non-smoker,
non-diabetic → **16.75%**) is row 0 of the test set, and the reference
reproduces it exactly. No CVrisk code is vendored. R was not installed at
design time, so the comparison against CVrisk covers that published test case,
not a run of CVrisk over the whole test set.

## The three versions

| Version | File | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | `design/reference.py` | The published formula in plain Python. The ground truth. |
| Twin | `design/twin.py` | A plaintext copy of the *encrypted* circuit: same slot layout, same rotations, same mask, same degree-27 curve stand-in, inputs and weights rounded to the 2^42 encoding scale. It predicts the encrypted result up to encryption noise. |
| FHE program | `keygen.cpp`, `encrypt.cpp`, `server.cpp`, `decrypt.cpp`, `common.hpp` | The encrypted implementation (OpenFHE CKKS). |

## Stage-by-stage decisions

**Stage 0 — environment.** A local build of this niobium-client checkout (no
Docker on the design machine). Target: the Niobium Fog FPGA profile (ring
dimension 2^16, CKKS, 62-bit prime cap).

**Stage 1 — privacy model.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Parties | **Patient** (client): their own measurements and the secret key. **Risk service** (server): the model's weights. |
| Who encrypts | Each patient encrypts only their own data, one question at a time (`per_record` packing). Different patients never share a ciphertext. |
| Who decrypts | The patient, with their own secret key. |
| Output integrity | Not needed: the patient who decrypts is the one who uses the result, so there is nobody to mislead. No transciphering. |
| Adversary | A semi-honest service that runs the protocol correctly but would like to read the data. |

Where each step runs:

| Step | Placement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Range check, logarithms of age/BMI/SBP, slot placement by sex and BP treatment | patient, in the clear | It is the patient's own data, and the logarithms are FHE-hostile; doing them client-side costs nothing. |
| Weighted sum, per-sex constant, risk curve | **service, encrypted** | The sensitive core: it combines the patient's data with the service's model. |
| Percentage and risk band | patient, in the clear, after decrypt | Presentation of the patient's own result. |

**Stage 2 — feasibility.** Data-oblivious: yes. Choosing the male or female
formula is handled by *where* the patient puts the numbers, so the service
never branches on data. One lane: arithmetic only. Depth: 8 against a
no-bootstrap ceiling of about 25. Slot use: 16 of 32,768 slots, the usual
cost of one patient per ciphertext; acceptable because each query is for one
person.

**Stage 3 — plaintext algorithm.** The published model is

    risk = 1 − S0^exp(L − mean),  L = Σ βᵢ · xᵢ  (x = ln age, ln BMI, ln SBP, smoker, diabetic)

with sex-specific β, `mean` and baseline survival `S0`. The key restructuring:

    S0^exp(z) = exp(−exp(z + ln(−ln S0)))

so with `u = L − mean + ln(−ln S0)` **both sexes share one curve**,
`risk = g(u) = 1 − exp(−exp(u))`. The sex-specific constant becomes a weight on
a slot that always holds 1, so the whole model is one weight vector and one
curve, with no branch.

**Stage 4 — scheme.** CKKS (approximate real arithmetic). A risk percentage
needs no exact integers, and CKKS is what the Fog runs.

**Stage 5 — circuit.** Slot layout (16 slots; every other slot is zero):

| Slots | Content |
|---|---|
| 0–7 | male block: ln age, ln BMI, ln SBP·(1−t), ln SBP·t, smoker, diabetic, 1, 0 |
| 8–15 | female block, same order |

The patient fills only their own block (t = 1 if blood pressure is treated);
the other block is all zeros. The service:

1. multiplies by the plaintext weight vector (both blocks, bias in slots 6 and
   14) — 1 level;
2. rotate-and-sum by 1, 2, 4, 8 — slot 0 now holds `u`; 0 levels, 4 rotation keys;
3. multiplies by a slot-0 mask — 1 level. Without it, slots 1–15 and the
   wrapped tail hold partial sums (for example the age term without its
   negative constant) that fall far outside the curve's range, where the
   stand-in polynomial diverges and would make the whole result undecodable;
4. evaluates `g` with a degree-27 Chebyshev interpolant on [−7, 3] — 6 levels;
5. compresses to one tower for the trip back.

**Range of `u`, known in advance.** Every weight on a log input or yes/no flag
is positive, so `u` is monotone in each input and its extremes sit at the
corners of the input box. Over the box (age 30–74, BMI 15–50, SBP 90–200)
`u ∈ [−5.654, 1.914]`; the curve covers [−7, 3], a margin of about 1.3 below
and 1.1 above. This bound depends on the input contract the patient's side
enforces, not on the test patients sampled.

**Stage 6 — parameters.**

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ring dimension N | 2^16 | the Fog's ring; 32,768 slots |
| Multiplicative depth | 8 | 1 weights + 1 mask + 6 curve |
| Scaling / first modulus | 42 / 57 bits | 15 bits of headroom at decrypt for a result in [0, 1] |
| log₂ Q | ≈ 57 + 8 × 42 = 393 bits (plus key-switching primes) | far below the ~1,780-bit 128-bit-security ceiling at 2^16 |
| Security | `HEStd_128_classic` | real 128-bit security at no extra cost |
| Scaling technique | FLEXIBLEAUTO | |
| Keys | relinearization + rotations {1, 2, 4, 8} | |

Out-of-range inputs: **refused and re-asked** (the user's choice at the
Stage 7 gate), not capped. Capping would always answer, but would understate
extreme values.

**Stage 7 — twin gate.** Degree sweep on 5,129 test patients (5,000
synthetic, every one of the 128 corners of the input box, and the CVrisk
case). Synthetic distributions are roughly primary-care-shaped and not fitted
to any cohort.

| Curve degree | Depth | Worst error vs reference (pp) | Risk-band changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 7 | 0.112 | 19 |
| 21 | 8 | 0.0035 | 0 |
| **27** | **8** | **0.0001** | **0** |
| 41 | 9 | < 0.000001 | 0 |

Degrees 21 and 27 cost the same depth; 27 is about 30× more accurate, so it
was chosen. Approved by the user before any encrypted code was written.

Encryption-noise tolerance recorded for the encrypted run: **|FHE − twin| ≤
0.001 percentage points (10⁻⁵ absolute) and zero risk-band changes**, a tenth
of the last digit CVrisk reports.

### Implementation notes (Stage 8 addendum)

- **Depth 7 → 8.** The first encrypted run failed with "multiplicative depth
  [7] is insufficient". `EvalChebyshevFunction` spends one level mapping the
  input onto [−1, 1] in addition to `ceil(log₂(degree+1))` for the series, so
  the degree-27 curve takes 6 levels, not the 5 in OpenFHE's
  `FUNCTION_EVALUATION.md` table. The twin's level count was corrected and the
  sweep rerun; the choice of degree 27 is unchanged.
- **Record once, replay many.** The server uses cooperative auto-tagging
  (`enable_auto_tagging()`, linking `niobium_client_autofacade`). The first
  query records the trace. Every later query finds it cached, runs zero FHE
  operations, and `replay()` notices that `ct_x.bin` changed on disk
  (`refresh_stale_inputs`) and loads the new patient onto the recorded
  addresses. Verified on patients with different sexes and inputs.
- **Simulator path.** In this checkout the installed
  `vendor/lib/niobium-client/bin/fhetch_sim` has no `LC_RPATH` and cannot
  load `libnbfhetch`, so `run_test.sh` and the demo point `NBCC_FHETCH_SIM`
  at the build-tree simulator.

## Protocol

**Setup (once per patient key set).**

| Direction | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| patient → service | crypto context `cc.bin`, public key `pk.bin`, relinearization key `mk.bin`, rotation keys `rk.bin` | 0.001 + 9.4 + 37.8 + 151.0 MB |

**Per question.**

| Direction | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| patient → service | one ciphertext `ct_x.bin` (16 used slots) | 9.4 MB |
| service → patient | one compressed ciphertext `ct_result.bin` | 1.05 MB |

Files that live only on the patient's machine: `sk.bin` and the plain
measurements. Files on the service: the context, public and evaluation keys,
`model.txt`, the latest ciphertext, and the trace cache. The service refuses
to start if `sk.bin` is present in its home, and its HTTP front end only
accepts the four setup files by name. The service home can be copied to
untrusted infrastructure as-is.

## What each party learns

- **The service** learns that a question was asked, when, and the ciphertext
  sizes, all of which are fixed. It does not learn any input, the patient's
  sex (both blocks are always present, one of them encrypted zeros), or the
  score.
- **The patient** learns their own score. By asking many questions they could
  work out the weights (model extraction). For this model that leaks nothing,
  because the weights are published; a service with a proprietary model would
  want rate limiting or coarser output.
- **Range refusal** happens on the patient's machine before anything is sent,
  so the service does not see refused questions at all.

## Threats not addressed

A malicious service that computes something other than the model (FHE gives no
proof of correct computation); timing side channels on the service host;
collusion between the service and anyone holding the patient's secret key;
compromise of the patient's own machine.
