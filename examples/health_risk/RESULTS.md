# Health-risk example: results

_Generated by the Niobium FHE Application Design AI Assistant (FHEanna)._

Measured on 2026-10-21 on an Apple-silicon Mac (local niobium-client Release
build), ring dimension 2^16, 128-bit security, every mode.

## How we know it passes

**The answer key.** The reference (`design/reference.py`) is the published
Framingham 2008 office-based formula. It reproduces the CVrisk R package's
published test case exactly (16.75%). For every test patient it gives the
expected risk, computed independently of any encryption work.

**The rehearsal.** The twin (`design/twin.py`) runs the exact encrypted
circuit in the clear: same slot layout, rotations, mask, degree-27 curve
stand-in and 2^42 rounding. Over all 5,129 test patients it stays within
0.0001 percentage points of the reference, with no patient in a different
risk band. So the design was known to be right before anything was encrypted.

**The test.** The same patients are encrypted on the patient's side, scored
blind by the server, decrypted, and compared against the twin's answer for
that patient. Across every run below, the largest difference is **0.00011
percentage points**, against a tolerance of 0.001 pp, and no patient changed
risk band.

**Why there is any difference at all.** Two small, budgeted effects. The risk
curve is replaced by a polynomial that matches it to about 10⁻⁶ (0.0001 pp).
Encryption adds a faint noise of the same order. Both were sized in advance.

**Why it is not a lucky run.** The same agreement holds on 100 ordinary
patients scored by replaying one recorded trace, on all 128 corners of the
input box (the extremes of the curve's range), and with real-math recording,
where the replayed result is **bit-identical** to OpenFHE's own.

**The honest caveat.** All of this shows the encrypted program faithfully
computes the published model. It says nothing about how well the Framingham
model predicts heart disease for a given population; that is the published
paper's claim, not this example's. The 5,000 random test patients are
synthetic and not fitted to any cohort.

## Deployment profile (measured)

| Stage | `--cpu` | `--sim` / `--sim-full` |
|---|---|---|
| keygen (once) | 0.7 s, 248 MiB | 0.7 s, 255 MiB |
| encrypt (per question) | 0.10 s, 98 MiB | 0.10 s, 96 MiB |
| server (per question) | 0.96 s, 358 MiB peak | 2.0 s, 561 MiB peak |
| decrypt (per question) | 0.08 s, 44 MiB | 0.08 s, 45 MiB |
| server, first question vs later | 0.99 s vs 0.96 s | 2.05 s vs 1.99 s (hollow); 2.82 s vs 2.00 s (real math) |

In the simulator modes the first question records the trace and every later
one replays it. Locally the difference is small because the hollow record is
cheap and the simulator replay (about 1.5 s for 17,535 instructions) is the
main cost.
